Case Study 01 · Research

SafeDeal: The Trust Layer for Social Commerce

"Nigerians don't have a payment problem; they have a trust problem." Buyers and sellers closing deals on WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook have no protection when a deal goes wrong. Bank transfer, Paystack and OPay move money reliably, but guarantee nothing about whether the other side keeps their word.

The evidence

  • 51%of Nigerian consumers hit by a financial scam in 12 months (Visa, 2026)
  • 57%of those scams happened on social media, the largest single channel
  • 3 of 5vendors we surveyed had lost sales to buyer distrust

What I did on the team

  • Market research. Sized the market top down (TAM ~US$9.5–13B, SAM ~US$2–4B social commerce) and labelled the SOM as an untested placeholder, because no Nigerian escrow adoption data exists.
  • Competitive analysis. A second research pass retired our own "nobody does this" claim: ~90+ escrow players operate in Nigeria, and TrustLink runs the same payment-link mechanic at 5–10%. Our edge had to come from execution: a lower fee, faster KYC, and staying inside the chat.
  • Sprint and backlog. Ran the backlog in Jira: 34 issues across 17 epics, from KYC to disputes to payouts.
  • Go-to-market strategy. Positioning: "the trusted middleman for Nigeria's chat commerce."

The key decision. CBN rules only let the ₦2B+ licence tier hold customer funds, and Paystack was fined ₦250M in 2025 over the wrong licence tier. So SafeDeal never holds money. Licensed rails (Paystack, Flutterwave) hold it; SafeDeal only triggers the release and earns a 3% fee. That one call removed our biggest regulatory risk.

Research, testing and honest gaps
  • User research. A survey of 20 shoppers (ages 21–40), 3 deep-dive interviews with empathy maps, and a 5-vendor survey. The sharpest finding: buyers find sellers on Jiji or Instagram, then move to WhatsApp to close, and lose all protection the moment they do.
  • Six rounds of live QA. We played vendor, buyer and admin and tried to break the build. We fixed a broken photo-upload bucket, a Google sign-in routing bug, and a KYC session-expiry bug that came back three times before we traced it to a token read without a refresh.
  • Gaps I'd close next. Seller-side interviews haven't been done yet, and that is the real two-sided cold-start risk. I also flagged that our buyer notes may read as stronger validation than they are, and asked for a second person to re-code them.

Team: Group 6, IdeateX 2026 PM Bootcamp. Irawooluwa Akintunde (Team Lead), Divine Agubosim (User Research), Boma George (Prototype), Emmanuel Kalasuwe (Market Research, GTM, Backlog).

Case Study 02 · Scoping

Family Fresh Basket: Build the Thinnest Possible V1

GrubNmore's core business is instant meal delivery. A weekend grocery basket, fresh produce and proteins with no decisions for the customer, was a guess at a second revenue line, not a proven one. The question wasn't "can we build this?" It was "should we build the full version before we know anyone wants it?"

What I chose not to build, and why

  • No Paystack API. Payment links are sent by hand from the Paystack dashboard. An API brings webhook failures, signature checks and retries that a product with no proven demand doesn't need yet.
  • No email service. Orders are confirmed on WhatsApp, where Lagos customers already are. One less system to break.
  • Fixed delivery windows. Friday to Sunday only. Orders placed Monday to Wednesday become pre-orders and get a payment link on Thursday morning, so operations never promise what they can't deliver.
  • Prices live in the database. A 7-row Supabase pricing table (core basket plus 6 add-ons) means a price change needs no developer and no deploy.

The PM point. Every cut traded completeness for speed to learn, and every cut can be reversed once real order volume justifies the extra engineering. Each one is written down in the README and the code, so a future engineer knows why the "obvious" full version wasn't built first.

Technical decisions I signed off on
  • Security model. The Supabase public key sits in the browser, which is safe only because Row Level Security is enforced on the server: pricing is read-only, and orders are insert-only from the client. The trade-off is written in the code, not left implied.
  • Stack chosen for speed, not scale. Next.js, TypeScript and Tailwind on Cloudflare Pages, with Supabase for data. That is a deliberate choice for a product with no revenue yet.
  • Repo hygiene. Real environment files are git-ignored and the example file holds placeholders only. I checked the full commit history: no secrets have ever been committed.

The full PRD, user flows, fulfilment process and success metrics live in a private product vault. The public repo is the implementation layer.

Case Study 03 · Live Product

Saamu: An Ajo Tool I Still Run Every Week

Rotating savings circles (ajo, esusu, adashe) are how many traders, tailors and small business owners in Nigeria build up a lump sum. But they run on memory, notebooks and WhatsApp groups. I coordinate a real 11-member weekly circle, so I built the tool to run it, and I still use it every week.

Designing around the real risk

The biggest risk in an ajo isn't payments. It's default risk: a member who collects the pot early and then stops paying. Everything in Saamu traces back to that one risk.

  • KYC and tiers. Members give identity and guarantor details, and a tier is calculated from how complete their KYC is.
  • Protected payout slots. The first two payout positions carry the most risk, so only fully verified Tier 3 members can hold them. The software enforces the rule, not my memory.
  • Multi-coordinator setup. Several coordinators can run their own circles on one system, kept apart by database-level security.

A business model call under a real constraint

OptionHow it worksVerdict
Path ADirect bank transfer plus WhatsApp receipts. Almost no fees, and money is available the same day.Chosen
Path BPaystack virtual accounts. Payments are matched automatically, but fees, VAT and payout costs eat most of the service fee.Rejected

Cost wasn't the deciding factor. Paystack settles the next working day, so a same-day Saturday payout would slip to Monday or Tuesday, and that would break trust with 11 real people. At this size, simple beat automated.

Bugs I found in production and fixed
  • The slot that silently disappeared. While testing the protected-slot rule, a 5-step check showed that moving a slot to a non-Tier 3 member wiped the original member instead of blocking the change. The cause was a delete and an insert running as two separate steps. The fix was one atomic database function. Checking the fix with raw SQL then exposed a second, display-only bug: the page showed "Unassigned" while the data was actually intact.
  • The green check that meant nothing. A scheduled job meant to stop the free database from pausing passed every time, and the database paused anyway. The full story is in Field Notes below.

Honest status: KYC is 100% complete for all 11 members, and at least one member has received a payout. On-time payment and default rates aren't reported yet, because the circle hasn't finished a full rotation.

Field Notes: Check the Thing, Not the Signal

Two incidents from products I own, with the same lesson. A green check or a busy dashboard is not proof. Go and look at what's actually there.

Saamu · Sep 2026

A Green CI Check That Meant Nothing

Symptom

A GitHub Actions job pinged Supabase every 3 days to stop the free database from pausing. It passed every time. The database paused anyway.

Root cause

The ping hit a health endpoint that never touches the database, so Supabase didn't count it as activity. A read from a dedicated heartbeat table also needed a table-level grant on top of its security policy.

The second miss

Two weeks later it paused again. A read still wasn't enough. I switched to a daily write and checked the timestamp itself before and after, instead of trusting a 200 response.

Lesson

My first "verification" was a false positive. The real fix was checking the proof, not just the fix.

GrubNmore · Sep 2026

A Busy Dashboard That Proved Nothing

Symptom

Hours after grubnmore.com launched, Google Search Console showed indexing and clicks going back weeks, before the site even existed.

Root cause

A domain-level property adds up every subdomain. The history belonged to grocery.grubnmore.com, an older product, not the new site.

Fix

I ran a Live Test on the real URL, matched the rendered page against the live homepage element by element, then requested indexing.

Lesson

I marked it "open, not verified" until Google's crawl completed. A dashboard showing data isn't a dashboard showing data about the thing you shipped.

From the Set to the Sprint

I didn't start in tech. I spent a decade making sure film and TV productions didn't fall apart. Then I decided to point that same discipline at software.

For over a decade, I worked in film and television production in Lagos. As Head of Camera Department at TiwaNTiwa Television, I was responsible for a camera inventory worth over ₦50 million with zero downtime. On every shoot day the gear had to work, be accounted for, and be where it needed to be, or the production lost money. At PlayTV I moved into production management and raised production efficiency by 40% and output by over 50%. Since 2019 I've worked as a freelance Director of Photography, producer and photographer on more than 20 independent projects.

None of that was "product management" on paper. Take away the film vocabulary and it's the same job: scope realistically against a fixed budget and deadline, protect quality when something threatens to slip, and make dozens of small calls correctly every day without anyone checking your work in real time.

In 2026 I completed the IdeateX Product Management bootcamp, where our team took SafeDeal to a hackathon final. Alongside it I run GrubNmore, a cloud kitchen in Lagos, working directly with a backend and a frontend developer as the founder in the middle who isn't the engineer. I'm not applying for PM roles because I already have the title. I'm applying because I've spent the last year proving I can do the work.
